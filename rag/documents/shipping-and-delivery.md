# Shipping, Tracking, and Delivery Exceptions

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy covers domestic shipments sent by Northstar Supply to a U.S.
address. It explains order handling, carrier estimates, tracking events, and
delivery investigations. It does not cover international customs, freight,
or orders fulfilled by marketplace sellers; route those cases to the relevant
specialist.

The dates in a customer's order and carrier record are authoritative for that
shipment. The estimates below are general service targets and must not be
presented as a guaranteed delivery date.

## 2. Fulfillment and transit are separate stages

- **Processing:** Time between order acceptance and carrier handoff. In-stock
  orders are usually prepared within 1–2 business days.
- **In transit:** Time after the carrier first scans the package. Ground
  delivery is commonly 3–7 business days after handoff, depending on distance
  and carrier capacity.
- **Delivery estimate:** A forecast from the carrier or checkout. Weather,
  peak volume, address corrections, and local disruptions can change it.

Business days are Monday through Friday, excluding U.S. federal holidays. A
weekend or holiday does not count toward the processing estimate.

## 3. Tracking events

### 3.1 Label created

“Label created” means shipment information was sent to the carrier. It does
not prove the carrier has the package. Allow up to 2 business days for the
first physical scan. If there is still no scan after that, support should
confirm fulfillment status with the warehouse.

### 3.2 In transit or delayed

Packages can go several days without a new scan while moving between carrier
facilities. If the carrier's delivery estimate has not passed, explain that
tracking has not updated and use the latest recorded event. Do not invent a
location or claim that the parcel is lost based on a scan gap alone.

If the estimate has passed and there has been no movement for 3 business days,
open a carrier investigation. Tell the customer the investigation has been
opened only after it is recorded in the support system.

### 3.3 Delivered scan, customer says not received

Ask the customer to check the delivery location, household members, building
mailroom, and nearby safe places. Allow 1 calendar day for a premature or
neighbor delivery scan to resolve. If the package remains missing, verify the
shipping address and open a carrier claim. Do not disclose another resident's
name or private delivery details.

## 4. Address changes and delivery holds

An address can be edited only before the warehouse marks the order as packed.
Once a carrier has possession, the customer may be able to request a carrier
intercept, but availability and fees depend on the carrier. Support must not
promise that an intercept will succeed.

For a request to change an address after shipment, verify the requester's
account using the approved workflow. Never redirect a package based solely on
an unauthenticated chat message. If the change cannot be made, explain the
available carrier options or wait for return-to-sender processing.

## 5. Split shipments and partial delivery

One order may ship in multiple packages. Each package has its own tracking
number and delivery events. Compare the package list with the order before
classifying an item as missing. If one package is delivered and another is
still moving within its estimate, provide the second package's latest status.

If all package records show delivered but an item is absent, treat the case as
a missing-item investigation. Do not mark the whole order lost merely because
one package is delayed.

## 6. Failed delivery and return to sender

Carriers may record an attempted delivery when access is unavailable, a
signature is required, or an address is incomplete. The customer should use
the carrier's notice or tracking page to arrange redelivery or pickup.

If the package is returned to the merchant, wait until warehouse receipt is
recorded before issuing a refund or reshipment. A return-to-sender scan alone
does not mean the merchant has received the item. Check the order and refund
records before describing the outcome.

## 7. Shipping charges and remedies

Shipping charges, replacements, and refunds are governed by the order record
and the Returns, Refunds, and Refund Timing policy. A delayed delivery does
not automatically qualify for a shipping-fee refund. If the package is
confirmed lost, support may offer a replacement or refund after checking
inventory, payment state, and duplicate-delivery risk.

Do not issue both a replacement and a full refund for the same shipment unless
a supervisor authorizes it. If a replacement is sent and the original package
later arrives, follow the recorded case instructions rather than promising
that the customer may keep both.

## 8. Privacy and verification

Before changing a destination or exposing detailed shipment information,
follow the account verification workflow. Do not reveal full street addresses,
phone numbers, or recipient details to someone who has not passed verification.
Never ask the customer to provide a password, one-time code, or full payment
card number in chat.

## 9. Escalation criteria

Escalate when a package is reported stolen, the carrier claim is disputed, the
address was changed without authorization, the package contains a safety
hazard, or tracking conflicts with the warehouse record. A carrier estimate
alone is not grounds to promise compensation.

## 10. Examples

- **Label created this morning:** Explain that the carrier may need up to 2
  business days to make its first scan; check warehouse status if there is no
  scan after that interval.
- **No movement for 1 day, estimate still in the future:** Report the latest
  scan and estimate. Do not call it lost.
- **Delivery estimate passed, no scan for 4 business days:** Open an
  investigation, then tell the customer it has been opened.
- **One of two parcels delivered:** Check the other tracking number before
  opening a missing-order claim.

## 11. Policy precedence

The shipment's carrier events and the merchant's fulfillment records determine
what happened to a specific parcel. This document supplies general handling
rules only. A written service guarantee attached to a particular order, if
present, takes precedence for that order.
